Q: Describe a choice you made when shipping a product or working on a technical challenge that didn't come from a product manager or an engineering manager/lead. How did you advocate for it, and what impact did it have on your end users? (optional)

At Coordinator the in-app assistant sent every request through a full agent loop. Nobody asked me to change that; the loop worked. But simple requests felt slow, and I suspected many didn't need tools at all.

I timed both paths on real requests. A single-shot path was 4-6x faster for many of them. I made the case with those numbers: keep the loop for requests that need tools, and add a single-shot tier for the rest.

We shipped it as a fourth tier, so users got answers to simple requests several times faster and the agent loop was kept for the requests that needed it. The habit I took from it: when something is slow, measure real requests before deciding the architecture is right.

Q: What's something, in a professional context, that you've gone deep on purely because you couldn't let it go? (optional)

Making speech services run on ARM64 NVIDIA hardware at Coordinator. We moved our self-hosted voice services (Whisper speech-to-text and a text-to-speech engine) onto ARM64 GPUs, and the TTS engine we had been using didn't carry over cleanly.

I couldn't let it go. I benchmarked TTS engines on ARM64 for latency and compatibility, swapped engines on the data, and got the voice path running on the new hardware. That depth paid off when we shipped production voice messaging in the web app: I understood where the latency went and what each engine traded away.
